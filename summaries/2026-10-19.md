### Documents: Police officer gave her Flock login to her ICE agent husband - (Hacker News)

https://www.wjcl.com/article/savannah-police-officer-ice-agent-flock-access/74135816

# Summary

A Savannah police officer, Amber Harpe, was fired after providing her husband—an Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agent—with unauthorized access to the police department's Flock license plate reader system. Documents reveal that her husband used her credentials to conduct nearly 1,000 searches across six states between January and July 2026, targeting individuals with active arrest warrants. While both claimed the searches were for legitimate law enforcement purposes related to ICE investigations, the Savannah Police Department determined that sharing login credentials violated department policy and compromised system security and accountability measures.

Department leadership disagreed on appropriate punishment, with one supervisor recommending an eight-hour suspension, but Major Michelle Halford and Assistant Police Chief Robert Gavin ultimately recommended termination, citing concerns that the arrangement undermined public trust and allowed unauthorized bypass of security protocols. The searches disproportionately involved Hispanic vehicle owners, raising additional concerns about using city resources for federal immigration enforcement without authorization. Harpe appealed the termination but withdrew her Civil Service Board appeal in late September.

### Timestral: Mistral release time-series foundation models - (Hacker News)

https://mistral.ai/news/timestral/

# Summary

Mistral has released Timestral, a family of time-series foundation models designed for forecasting across enterprise applications. The largest model contains just 209M parameters yet ranks first on all three major forecasting benchmarks (GIFT-Eval, TIME, and fev-bench), outperforming larger competitors. Available in four sizes (5M to 209M parameters), Timestral balances accuracy and efficiency, running on standard CPUs and excelling at zero-shot forecasting across diverse domains without requiring retraining on new data.

The model's key innovations include decoupled horizon forecasting, covariate-aware attention mechanisms, and synthetic-first pre-training followed by real-data post-training. Timestral supports probabilistic multivariate forecasts with categorical covariates and known-future information, making it practical for real-world applications like industrial monitoring and financial forecasting. Organizations can further adapt the models to their specific domains through Mistral Forge, which offers fine-tuning and full control over training data while maintaining zero-shot capabilities.

### Show HN: Qwen3-4B with the AI writing tics trained out - (Hacker News)

https://100toolkit.com/blog/deslop-model/

# Summary

Erik Markus Kannike fine-tuned Qwen3-4B to eliminate common AI writing habits—such as em dashes, "not X, but Y" constructions, and lists of three—through a three-round distillation process trained on a MacBook Air. The resulting model, qwen3-4b-deslop, reduced the "slop score" from 121 to 12.5 per 1,000 words (human writing typically scores under 15) while maintaining factual accuracy and genre adherence. The 2.5 GB model is available for local use via Ollama.

However, the model has notable limitations: it produces flat, declarative writing and still invents names and places despite eliminating most fabricated studies. The author plans to improve future versions by training on high-quality human writing examples and scaling to larger models (7-30B parameters), followed by blind reader testing. The current release demonstrates that targeted fine-tuning can remove stylistic AI artifacts without severely compromising model performance.

### Alternatives to Animal Testing Are Finally Taking Off - (Hacker News)

https://spectrum.ieee.org/alternatives-to-animal-testing

# Summary

Scientists have developed promising technologies called NAMs (new approach methodologies) to replace animal testing in drug development and toxicology research, with organ-on-chip systems being among the most advanced. These devices, such as Harvard's lung-on-a-chip developed 17 years ago, use human cells to simulate organ function and have proven effective at predicting drug reactions better than animal models, which fail to predict outcomes in approximately 92 percent of clinical trials. The FDA has even begun requiring these alternatives instead of animal data for some drug trials.

However, the real challenge to widespread adoption is not technological but organizational—requiring changes in policy, training, culture, and standardization rather than engineering improvements. While NAMs show significant potential and are becoming more sophisticated, implementing them at scale requires what experts call "change management" more than technological advancement, as the scientific and pharmaceutical communities must validate these systems, establish standards, and fundamentally shift decades-old practices that have relied on animal models as the default approach.

### Let's Encrypt is adding two additional remote perspectives for domain validation - (Hacker News)

https://community.letsencrypt.org/t/lets-encrypt-is-adding-two-additional-remote-perspectives-for-domain-validation/252166

# Summary

Let's Encrypt is expanding its domain validation security by adding two new remote perspectives, increasing the total from four to six remote validation points plus one primary datacenter (seven total). This change is required by the Baseline Requirements effective December 15, 2026, which mandate at least five remote network perspectives to improve protection against BGP attacks and enhance domain validation security.

The implementation will begin with staging testing on October 27th, 2026, followed by a production rollout on November 10th, 2026. During validation, both the primary server and a quorum of remote perspectives must receive correct challenge responses for issuance to proceed, making it significantly harder for attackers to hijack the validation process. The change is expected to have minimal impact on users.

### Wine Ported to OpenBSD - (Hacker News)

https://marc.info/?l=openbsd-ports&m=179236125692175

# Summary

Thomas Frohwein announces a new Wine port for OpenBSD, representing a collaborative effort to enable Windows software compatibility on the platform. The project overcame a significant technical hurdle regarding %gs register handling through an emulation solution, and has successfully tested dozens of 64-bit Windows applications, including recent titles like The Witcher 3 Remaster. The port demonstrates growing interest in Wine compatibility, particularly as modern Windows software has increasingly shifted to 64-bit architecture.

The main limitation is that 32-bit Windows software (WoW64) is not yet supported, though 64-bit applications run successfully. Built-in tests are currently disabled due to technical challenges with X server access and hanging tests, though real-world gaming has not shown these issues. The author recommends users test the port with applications like Notepad++ and suggests running Wine as a non-privileged user for security purposes while additional sandboxing measures are explored.

### Harness Engineering 101: The LLM is the brain, the harness is the body - (Hacker News)

https://github.com/IsuruMaduranga/harness-engineering-101

# Harness Engineering 101 Summary

This GitHub repository contains "Harness Engineering 101," a comprehensive educational resource that teaches AI agent development from first principles. The project uses a central metaphor: the LLM serves as the "brain" while the "harness" is the "body"—the program that builds, maintains, and manages the conversation array sent to the LLM API. Rather than treating agents as complex systems, the series demonstrates that all agent patterns are essentially patches to one fundamental loop, each created to solve specific problems as they arise.

The resource is structured as a 16-chapter book (published September 2026) with accompanying runnable Python code, organized into four parts: "The Wire" (core mechanics), "Running Long" (handling context limits), "The Ecosystem" (frameworks and extensions), and "Trust, Domains, and Data" (safety and specialization). The content progresses systematically through real failures and minimal solutions, making it accessible for developers building production AI agents. Readers can access the rendered book online, explore the source chapters on GitHub, or run the toy harness locally using the provided code.

### ESA's Hera Will View Asteroid Scars from DART's Strike - (Hacker News)

https://spectrum.ieee.org/esa-hera-dimorphos-rendezvous

# Summary

The European Space Agency's Hera mission is set to rendezvous with the binary asteroid pair Dimorphos-Didymos in November 2026 to document the aftermath of NASA's DART impact test from 2022. Launched in October 2024, Hera will capture detailed images and measurements of the asteroids, particularly the crater and debris created when DART intentionally collided with Dimorphos at 6.6 km/s, which successfully shortened the asteroid's orbit by approximately 32 minutes—demonstrating a viable planetary defense strategy.

The mission will deploy two CubeSats (Juventas and Milani) to conduct further analysis of the asteroids' composition, dust fields, and internal structure, with Juventas potentially landing on Dimorphos itself. This follow-up investigation will provide crucial insights into asteroid deflection techniques that could prove essential if an Earth-threatening asteroid is ever discovered, while also advancing spacecraft technology through autonomous control demonstrations.

### Gdynia and the Dream of a Maritime Poland - (Hacker News)

https://engelsbergideas.com/notebook/gdynia-and-the-dream-of-a-maritime-poland/

# Summary

Gdynia embodies interwar Poland's modernization aspirations and its pursuit of maritime independence. Following World War I, Woodrow Wilson's Fourteen Points promised Poland access to the sea as a necessity for becoming a modern, independent nation. However, Poland faced a geographical challenge: squeezed between Germany and East Prussia with no viable ports, the country decided to construct its own. In 1921, work began transforming the small fishing village of Gdynia into a gleaming port city, complete with functionalist Bauhaus architecture and modern infrastructure. By the 1930s, it had become Poland's maritime hub, serving commercial, passenger, and naval purposes.

Yet Gdynia's construction, while symbolizing Poland's modernization and self-confidence, also revealed deeper contradictions. Thousands of Poles departed through its passenger terminals seeking better lives abroad, reflecting the country's uneven development and persistent poverty. Additionally, the city could not shield Poland from the looming threat of German expansionism or guarantee the security that Polish leaders believed maritime access would provide. Though Gdynia fulfilled part of Wilson's vision by providing Poland access to the Baltic, it ultimately could not deliver the lasting security and prosperity that its architects had envisioned.
