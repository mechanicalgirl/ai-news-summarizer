### OpenLogi - (Hacker News)

https://openlogi.org/en

# OpenLogi Summary

OpenLogi is a free, open-source alternative to Logitech's Options+ software that lets users configure their Logitech mice locally without requiring an account or sending telemetry. Written in Rust, it supports remapping buttons, controlling DPI settings, adjusting SmartShift functionality, and creating per-app profiles across multiple connection types (Bolt, Unifying, Lightspeed, Bluetooth, and USB) on macOS, Linux, and Windows.

The application prioritizes privacy and user control by storing all settings in a plain-text TOML configuration file on your machine, with no cloud synchronization or analytics tracking. It offers 44 built-in actions, an interactive configurator interface, and support for multiple Logitech devices including the MX Master series, MX Anywhere 3, and various keyboards and accessories—all completely independent from and unaffiliated with Logitech.

### Cerebras CS-4 - (Hacker News)

https://www.cerebras.ai/cs4

# Summary

Cerebras has unveiled the CS-4, a new AI accelerator system that delivers up to 30x faster inference speeds compared to GPU-based systems. The system features three WSE-3 Turbo wafers per unit and introduces the modular Nexus Rack-Scale Platform designed for hyperscale datacenter deployment. Key innovations include a compact wafer-scale backpack design with 50% fewer components, power delivery positioned just 0.5mm from the processor to eliminate board-level losses, and a programmable I/O subsystem that enables wafer-to-wafer latency as low as 2 microseconds—critical for maintaining interactive performance on models with tens of trillions of parameters.

The CS-4 is specifically engineered for hyperscale infrastructure, reducing deployment time from days to hours through its modular design that separates power, cooling, and networking layers from the compute components. The system achieves up to 10x more throughput per watt than its predecessor (CS-3) and can generate over 1,000 tokens per second on 10+ trillion parameter models, making it particularly well-suited for agentic AI applications. First CS-4 shipments begin this quarter.

### Scientists stunned by children's lung recovery in ultra low emission zone - (Hacker News)

https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c1l1r1zne1ro

# Summary

Researchers were "stunned" to discover that children's lung function rapidly improved after London's Ultra Low Emission Zone (ULEZ) was introduced in 2019. A five-year study of over 3,400 primary school children found that London children whose lung growth had been stunted by air pollution showed impressive recovery, with their lung capacity accelerating to match that of children in the less-polluted city of Luton by the study's end. The proportion of London children with clinically impaired lung capacity dropped from 14% to 9%, suggesting the policy successfully reduced harmful nitrogen dioxide emissions from vehicles.

Scientists believe this represents the strongest evidence yet that local clean air zones can reverse pollution-related damage to children's developing lungs, with potential lifelong health benefits. However, independent researchers caution that other factors—such as changes in walking or cycling to school, or broader pandemic-related effects—should also be considered. While the findings are promising and have global relevance given billions of children live in polluted cities, lead researchers emphasize that air pollution in London and across the UK remains above WHO guidelines, indicating further work is needed.

### Palomar: A registry of Lean verified mathematics - (Hacker News)

https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/08/18/palomar-a-registry-of-lean-verified-mathematics/

# Summary

Terence Tao announces the opening of Palomar, a new registry for Lean-verified mathematics developed by the Lean FRO and ICARM. Created to address concerns about AI-generated proofs, Palomar functions as a preprint server for formalized mathematical proofs, requiring submissions to include a human-readable challenge file, a proof module, and metadata describing results informally. The registry employs automated mechanical verification (using the Lean tool Comparator) to confirm that proofs typecheck and match their claims, along with AI-assisted semantic verification to ensure informal descriptions align with formal statements.

Importantly, Tao emphasizes that Palomar is not a peer-reviewed journal and does not conduct traditional review for novelty or rigor—its checks fall short of professional peer review. However, the submission process has proven manageable, as demonstrated by Tao's own successful submission of a formalization of Sendov's conjecture. The registry now welcomes submissions of both human-generated and AI-generated formalizations of old and new mathematical results.

### A 3D fruit fly on macOS desktop powered by the real FlyWire connectome - (Hacker News)

https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/desktop-fly

# Summary

Desktop-Fly is a macOS application that simulates a realistic 3D fruit fly living on your desktop, powered by an actual neural simulation of the fruit fly brain connectome from FlyWire. The project uses 23,210 real neuron positions and a 668-neuron circuit with ~19,000 real synaptic connections running at 1 kHz to drive the fly's behavior. The fly's actions—walking, grooming, sleeping, and escaping from cursor movements—are determined by genuine neural firing patterns, including escape responses triggered by the Giant Fiber neurons that spike in response to looming threats, replicating real fly behavior in approximately 4 milliseconds.

The application is permission-free, rendering the fly on a transparent overlay across your desktop while it interacts with windows, responds to cursor proximity, and follows circadian rhythms. Users can interact with a live visualization of the fly's brain, stimulating different neural regions to observe corresponding behaviors. The entire project is built in Swift for macOS 13+ and includes diagnostic tools to verify neural circuit accuracy and behavioral responses.

### Solo – a .so loader for static Linux binaries - (Hacker News)

https://github.com/pg83/solo

# Summary

**SoLo** is a portable solution that enables fully static Linux binaries to dynamically load and use glibc-linked GPU drivers (Vulkan/OpenGL) at runtime without containers or AppImages. It works by providing a custom ELF loader and a glibc ABI bridge built on musl, allowing a single static executable to access the host's hardware-specific graphics drivers while keeping all other dependencies self-contained.

The project includes a fully functional Vulkan proof-of-concept that runs compute shaders and generates PNG output, tested across AMD, Intel, and NVIDIA GPUs. SoLo can be used as a standalone library or integrated into musl-static applications, with the core implementation small enough to be self-contained and copyable into other build systems. CI validation confirms compatibility by loading over 2,100 host shared objects from Debian's most-installed packages on both x86-64 and aarch64 architectures.

### How does IKEA come up with names for its products? - (Hacker News)

https://www.ikea.com/se/en/customer-service/knowledge/articles/6f564c4d-2ccc-46de-b643-545a3948dc79.html

# Summary

IKEA founder Ingvar Kamprad started naming products as a practical solution because he had difficulty remembering numbers. This naming practice has evolved into a core part of IKEA's brand identity and Swedish heritage. The company follows two main rules: all product names must be real Swedish words organized by category (sofas use place names, bookshelves use men's names, children's products use animals), and names must be 4-12 letters, pronounceable, and checked to avoid undesirable meanings in other languages. Non-Swedish names are reserved for services and functions that require clarity for all global customers.

IKEA names approximately 2,000-3,000 new products annually using this systematic approach, which creates a distinctive and playful brand identity while maintaining consistency across markets.
