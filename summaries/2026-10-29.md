### How to Make Artificial Intelligence More Environmentally Sustainable - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/AI-environmentally-sustainable/

# Summary

The IT industry, responsible for 2-4% of global emissions, faces growing environmental challenges as AI development significantly increases electricity consumption. Major tech companies like Microsoft and Google have reported substantial emissions increases—Microsoft up 30% since 2020 and Google up 50% since 2019—primarily due to AI infrastructure and data centers. Global data center electricity consumption alone reaches 700 terawatt-hours annually, equivalent to Brazil's total energy usage, making sustainability a critical concern comparable to the aviation industry's environmental impact.

To address this, experts recommend treating environmental sustainability as a top-level architectural consideration alongside cost and performance. Key strategies include using pre-built and smaller models instead of training from scratch, running computations in regions with abundant low-carbon electricity, optimizing job execution time, and shifting workloads to times and locations where renewable energy is available. Training on 100% renewable energy can reduce carbon emissions by 45-99%, making strategic energy sourcing and infrastructure choices essential for building environmentally responsible AI systems.

### Datadog Builds a Specialist AI Model for SRE Investigations - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/datadog-scout-sre-model/

# Summary

Datadog has released Scout, a specialized AI model designed to investigate production incidents within its Bits Investigation product. Scout is trained to analyze alerts, examine telemetry data, and identify root causes of problems. The model achieves performance comparable to leading general-purpose AI models (GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude Opus) while costing 3-8 times less to run, making it economically viable to deploy as an "always-on first responder" that can begin investigations before engineers are engaged.

Key technical innovations addressed during training included adjusting reward structures to encourage thorough investigation and supporting evidence rather than premature conclusions. Early testing shows Scout resolves 71-80 cases on a public benchmark depending on effort level, positioning it competitively within the emerging market of AI-assisted incident response tools.

### Airbnb Cuts Recommendation Staleness from 2 Days to Seconds with Chronon - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/airbnb-chronon-recommendation/

# Summary

Airbnb has significantly improved its recommendation system by extending its open-source Chronon feature platform with Push Mode and Near Real-Time (NRT) Model Transform capabilities. These additions enable the system to update recommendation embeddings within seconds of guest activity, reducing staleness from nearly two days to under a minute. The improvement delivered a 1.67% gain in offline performance metrics and a 0.33% increase in uncancelled bookings.

Previously, Airbnb's JourneyFormer sequence recommender ran as a daily batch job, leaving guest activity unrepresented until the next day's run. The new architecture uses streaming to detect feature updates and triggers model inference immediately, writing fresh embeddings to the same low-latency store used by the batch system. This approach maintains consistency between offline and online computation while keeping the serving path largely unchanged, demonstrating a practical pattern where predictions update based on feature changes rather than being computed for every request.

### Chrome Opens Trial for the Email Verification API: Letting Sites Verify Without Sending Emails - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/chrome-email-verification/

# Summary

Google has launched a Chrome origin trial for the Email Verification API, a new authentication method that eliminates the need for users to receive verification emails. Instead of the traditional flow of entering an email, retrieving a code from an inbox, and returning to complete signup, the browser acts as a mediator between the website and the user's email provider (like Gmail). If the user is already logged into their email provider, the browser automatically retrieves a signed token and completes verification without sending any messages, reducing context-switching that often causes users to abandon signup.

The implementation is relatively simple for developers—requiring an autocomplete hint and hidden input field on the frontend—though server-side validation of the token signature is more involved. While some developers praise the approach for eliminating a major friction point in registration, critics argue the privacy benefits are overstated and question whether adding browser-level identity complexity solves genuine problems. The API currently works with Gmail in Chrome 150 and later, with a fallback to traditional email verification for users not signed into their providers.

However, the service is constrained to a 1 MB maximum per namespace with at most 10,000 key-value pairs and only one write per namespace per second, making it purpose-built for read-heavy, infrequently-updated data.

This design targets configuration management scenarios like feature flags, routing tables, and agent settings where ultra-low latency reads matter more than write flexibility, making it ideal for teams needing instant global configuration propagation without the overhead of classic KV's higher limits.

### Uber Moves beyond CPU Cores with Performance-Aware Scheduling for Stateful Workloads - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/uber-cpu-stateful-workloads/

# Summary

Uber has introduced Normalized Compute Units (NCUs) within its Odin orchestration platform to optimize CPU allocation across its heterogeneous server fleet of approximately 5 million cores. Rather than treating all CPU cores equally, the system assigns performance factors to different server types (with newer hardware receiving factors up to 190 compared to a baseline of 100), accounting for the fact that single-threaded performance can vary by nearly 2x between the fastest and slowest processors. This allows workloads to receive the appropriate number of physical cores based on server capability rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.

This performance-aware scheduling is particularly critical for stateful workloads, which cannot be easily load-balanced across servers like stateless services. For these systems, a workload moving from fast to slow hardware could see substantial CPU consumption increases, risking resource contention. After implementing NCUs across 1.4 million cores, Uber reduced CPU utilization variance between server types from 8 percentage points to 3 percentage points and achieved an 8% overall reduction in CPU allocation, demonstrating significant efficiency gains.

### How Anthropic Redesigned Source Control for AI-Driven Development - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/anthropic-source-control/

# Summary

Anthropic recently migrated its monorepo from Git to Scion, a custom source control system designed for AI-driven development at scale. Scion moves repository operations to the server side, using content-addressed storage and tiered caches to handle Anthropic's explosive growth from 2,500 daily merged changes in May to over 30,000 in September. The system eliminates the need for offline independence, instead relying on shared infrastructure to manage a 100 GB monorepo with approximately 25 million daily CI checkouts.

Scion achieves significant performance improvements by storing commits, merges, and history queries on servers while clients maintain bounded caches with virtualized checkouts that materialize files on demand. The tiered cache architecture (node-local, regional, and authoritative) achieves over 99% cache-hit rates and delivers 200 times smaller infrastructure footprint and two orders of magnitude faster checkouts compared to Git. However, Anthropic notes that Scion is not a general-purpose Git replacement and recommends adopting specific ideas—such as server-side APIs and improved fetch protocols—into existing Git platforms rather than wholesale system replacement.

### AWS Shows Use of AI Agents to Triage Linux Kernel CVEs - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/aws-ai-agents-linux-kernel-cves/

# Summary

AWS engineers presented their process for triaging the massive influx of Linux kernel CVEs affecting EC2 infrastructure. After the Linux kernel became a CVE Numbering Authority in 2024, CVE volume jumped from ~2 per week to ~16 per day. AWS uses a three-stage approach: an automated script that filters out ~70% of CVEs based on kernel version and configuration, an AI agent that classifies remaining CVEs by subsystem and vulnerability type in ~3.4 seconds each, and human review by two engineers. The team estimates the AI agent has a 5-10% error rate and emphasizes they will not rely solely on LLM output for security decisions.

The team also advocates for moving to newer long-term-support (LTS) kernels, as their data shows older branches accumulate unfixed CVEs over time—for example, Linux 5.10 had 1,709 unfixed CVEs compared to 229 in 6.18 as of September 2026. AWS has accelerated its kernel upgrade cycles, reducing the time from 4.19 to 5.10 (two years) to the 6.18 upgrade (six months), though they note that backporting fixes poses risks if prerequisite commits are missed.

The article identifies five distinct user archetypes on infrastructure teams using AI tools in 2026—ranging from aggressive "soloists" shipping 40 pull requests daily to cautious "tourists" who periodically revisit the technology. While these users represent different adoption patterns along Rogers's diffusion curve, the key distinction lies not in *when* they adopt AI, but in *how* they use it and the risks they create for their teams.

These practices provide the essential "framing" that allows teams to move quickly without creating systems nobody can understand, similar to how jazz musicians improvise effectively within agreed-upon chord changes.
