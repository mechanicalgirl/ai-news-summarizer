### The Emergent Symbolic Structure of Artificial Neural Networks - (Hacker News)

https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29530

# Summary

This paper investigates how neural networks, despite using continuous vector representations, can excel at tasks traditionally thought to require symbolic reasoning. The researchers propose that neural networks implicitly develop symbolic structure internally, and they demonstrate this by showing that various neural networks' representations can be accurately approximated using closed-form equations that instantiate symbolic structures. Their findings span from small networks manipulating lists to large language models operating in symbolic domains like arithmetic, logic, code, and language.

The authors further validate their hypothesis by using their identified symbolic structures to make targeted modifications to an LLM's behavior, confirming that the model's performance depends on these underlying symbolic representations. This work bridges the long-standing divide between symbolic AI (which traditionally models intelligence as logical operations on symbols) and modern neural network-based AI, suggesting that both paradigms may actually be describing the same phenomenon from different perspectives.

### LLM Judges Verify Presence, Not Absence: Omission Blindness in AI Clinical Notes - (Hacker News)

https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31016

# Summary

This paper reveals a critical limitation in using LLMs as judges to verify AI-generated clinical notes: they effectively detect *added or altered* information but fail to reliably detect *omissions* (missing information from clinical encounters). Testing eight judge designs on a benchmark of 500 note pairs, the researchers found discrimination accuracy of 0.79-0.94 for detecting additions/alterations but only 0.50-0.63 for omissions—barely better than chance.

The authors recovered omission detection by restructuring the task: instead of having the judge directly compare notes to transcripts, they had it first extract all facts from the transcript, then systematically check whether each fact appears in the note. Two independent approaches achieved this—a per-fact pipeline and a single-prompt method evolved via GEPA optimization—with the single-call method detecting more omissions (36.9% vs. 24.6%) at half the false-alarm rate, despite costing ten times less. Physician validation confirmed the pipeline's superiority on disagreements, suggesting this factual extraction approach provides a practical solution to omission blindness in clinical AI auditing.

### Why are there no flow batteries with symmetric ferrocyanide electrolytes? - (Hacker News)

https://chemisting.com/2026/09/02/why-are-there-no-flow-batteries-with-symmetric-ferrocyanide-electrolytes/

# Summary

While ferrocyanide/ferricyanide redox couples are excellent for asymmetric flow batteries due to their high stability, great kinetics, and solubility, they cannot be used in symmetric systems (where both electrodes start with identical electrolyte). The primary reason is that ferrocyanide decomposes under the extreme potentials required in symmetric batteries, breaking down into free cyanide and iron metal, which then form insoluble Prussian blue and other harmful compounds that destroy the battery.

Additionally, ferrocyanide is unstable when exposed to potentials more than approximately ±1V away from its redox potential, which makes it unsuitable for economically viable flow batteries. The author strongly warns against attempting symmetric ferrocyanide systems outside of professional laboratory settings due to the significant safety hazard posed by the free cyanide generated during electrochemical decomposition.

### It's OK to hardcode feature flags (2025) - (Hacker News)

https://code.mendhak.com/hardcode-feature-flags/

# Summary

This article argues that hardcoding feature flags is often a perfectly valid and underrated approach compared to dedicated feature flag management software. The author contends that while such management systems are heavily marketed, they introduce unnecessary complexity, infrastructure overhead, security risks, and non-deterministic behavior that makes code harder to reason about. Hardcoded flags using simple JSON files read at startup are simpler, more reliable, and safer for most teams.

The author advocates for a pragmatic approach: start with hardcoded flags, manage them actively by removing obsolete ones, and only adopt feature flag management software when a team genuinely scales to the point of needing runtime changes at scale. This stance challenges the tech industry's tendency toward premature optimization and complexity, emphasizing that boring, straightforward solutions are often the best engineering choice.

### Fine, I'll build my own text editor - (Hacker News)

https://dbushell.com/2026/09/01/text-editor/

# Summary

David Bushell explores building a custom text editor from scratch, experimenting with three different approaches. He starts with a `<canvas>` element for complete control but discovers it's inaccessible and labor-intensive, requiring him to manually implement features like text selection and scrolling. He then tries `contenteditable="plaintext-only"`, which leverages browser accessibility features for free but encounters unpredictable performance issues with larger files.

His final approach uses a simple `<textarea>` element paired with syntax highlighting, which proves performant and accessible with minimal complexity. While acknowledging the project still lacks features like proper tab indentation, Bushell concludes that the `<textarea>` solution achieves "90% of a text editor with 1% of the features" and demonstrates that sometimes the simplest web standards are more practical than complex custom implementations.

### Open Battery Information - (Hacker News)

https://github.com/mnh-jansson/open-battery-information

# Summary

The Open Battery Information project provides tools to repair batteries by resetting Battery Management System (BMS) faults that may have been falsely triggered or are no longer relevant. Rather than discarding a functional battery when its BMS software flags a fault, this project allows users to diagnose and potentially restore the battery to working condition.

The project has transitioned from a desktop application to a modern web-based interface that runs in the browser. Users set up Arduino firmware on an Arduino Uno or ESP32-C3 microcontroller, which connects via USB to the OBI-1 web tool. The web tool allows users to read battery information including model, cell voltages, temperatures, and charge status without requiring any local software installation.

### FBI Probes Service Selling 153M+ Drivers Licenses - (Hacker News)

https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/09/fbi-probes-service-selling-153m-drivers-licenses/

# Summary

A new dark web identity theft service called Nexus is selling digital scans of over 153 million drivers licenses from the United States and Canada, along with millions of other identity documents. Through investigative work, journalist Brian Krebs traced the source to IDScan.net, a Louisiana-based identity verification company whose technology is used by major retailers like Hertz, Target, and marijuana dispensaries. By analyzing timestamps and cross-referencing with individuals' travel and rental car records, Krebs determined that the stolen data likely came from IDScan's ID scanning devices at car rental companies and marijuana dispensaries.

The FBI's New Orleans field office has launched an official investigation into the apparent breach at IDScan.net, which processes over 21 million identity verifications monthly at 20,000 locations worldwide. The stolen data includes not only drivers licenses but also government access cards and marijuana dispensary cards, and is being continuously updated with fresh stolen records. Security experts argue that companies handling such sensitive personal information need to be held to higher security standards.
