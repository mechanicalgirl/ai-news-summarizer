### ZuckOff Know when a camera is in the room - (Hacker News)

https://zuckoff.app/

# ZuckOff Summary

ZuckOff is a privacy-focused iOS and Android app that detects camera glasses in your vicinity by listening for their Bluetooth signals. The app identifies popular models like Ray-Ban Meta, Oakley Meta, and Snap Spectacles by their manufacturer signatures, displays evidence for each detection, and logs all nearby Bluetooth devices. It operates entirely on your phone with no account required, and users can mark their own glasses to prevent false alerts.

The app has limitations—it cannot detect glasses that stay silent when worn, and signal detection doesn't prove recording is occurring. The developers are actively seeking more camera glasses hardware to expand detection capabilities and welcome contributions from users and privacy groups. ZuckOff also offers branded merchandise, with proceeds supporting ongoing development.

### Disney+: New user agreement allows ads before movies in all subscriptions - (Hacker News)

https://consumerrights.wiki/w/Disney%2B_ad_policy_change

# Summary

In January 2025, Disney+ modified its Subscriber Agreement to allow advertisements across all subscription tiers, including premium "ad-free" plans that were previously marketed as completely advertisement-free. The updated terms permit ads in specific circumstances such as content with restricted streaming rights, live/linear programming, special events, and promotional messages about Disney bundles and branded content.

This policy change applies retroactively to all subscribers—including those who purchased premium tiers before the modification—who are bound by the new terms unless they cancel their subscription. The change represents a significant shift from Disney+'s original service offering, as subscribers who paid for ad-free experiences may now encounter advertisements despite their premium tier designation.

### Kev: Tiny Jev-like family of decision models built on top of Qwen3.5 - (Hacker News)

https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/tree/main

# Summary

Kev is an open-source project that provides small decision-making models (0.8B, 4B, and 9B parameters) built on Qwen3.5 that can be trained and run locally. The models are designed to answer yes/no, multiple-choice, and rating questions based on input text, with an API compatible with TypeSafe's System One. Users can download pretrained weights, run the models on CUDA or Apple Silicon, and experiment with a web playground interface.

The project includes comprehensive evaluation data showing that Kev-9B achieves competitive performance (0.852 accuracy on new sources) compared to the commercial Jev model, while offering the advantage of local deployment and customization. Installation is straightforward via Python, and the models support multiple question types in a single request while keeping questions isolated from each other's outputs.

### Jev-Leftpad - (Hacker News)

https://github.com/f/jev-leftpad

# Summary

This GitHub repository is a humorous npm package called `jev-leftpad` that left-pads strings using TypeSafe AI's Jev language model instead of using the built-in JavaScript `padStart()` function. The package makes an API call to Jev for each operation, asking it to choose from options (space_0 through space_10) to determine how many spaces to add, making it an unnecessarily complex and expensive solution to a trivial problem.

The creator explicitly acknowledges this is impractical, noting that it costs more than the standard method, can fail or choose incorrectly, and is limited to adding 0-10 spaces. The license and documentation humorously advise against using it in production or anything important, indicating this is a joke project showcasing the absurdity of using AI for simple tasks.

### ZuckOff Is a Free App That Sees Meta Glasses Before They See You - (Hacker News)

https://www.wired.me/story/meta-smart-glasses-detector-app-zuckoff

# Summary

ZuckOff, a free Bluetooth detection app created by Polish developer Pawel Szydlowski, helps users identify Meta smart glasses in their vicinity by detecting their unique digital fingerprints. The app has gained traction following growing privacy concerns about unsolicited recording with smart glasses, particularly after a BBC investigation revealed dozens of accounts posting content filmed with Meta glasses without consent. With approximately seven million pairs of Meta smart glasses sold in 2025, the app addresses a real public safety issue as venues like schools and cinemas increasingly ban their use.

However, ZuckOff has significant limitations—it cannot determine if glasses are actively recording or identify who is wearing them, only providing rough proximity information. The app works by reading Bluetooth signals and is difficult for Meta to legally challenge since it doesn't intercept data. While this tool makes an "invisible problem" partially visible, the article suggests that comprehensive solutions will ultimately require legislative action rather than consumer detection apps.

### Show HN: Lossless-memory – a personal AI memory that never summarizes - (Hacker News)

https://github.com/aru-labs/lossless-memory

# Summary

**Lossless-Memory** is a local, file-based long-term memory system for personal AI assistants that prioritizes keeping complete conversation records over summarization. Instead of condensing interactions into compact notes, it stores every line verbatim with precise timestamps, organized around a "Temporal Backbone" that treats time as the primary search axis. Users can query conversations using time-based phrases (e.g., "yesterday evening, about the budget") combined with keyword search, retrieving exact conversation passages in chronological order rather than paraphrased summaries.

The system uses three core components: raw JSONL logs as the source of truth, a SQLite full-text index tied to timestamps for word-based search within time ranges, and a lightweight "LLL" index that tracks current conversation topics to maintain context across sessions. Built for single-user operation since July 2026, it demonstrates that this approach is practical and memory-efficient, with semantic search serving only as a fallback when exact matching yields insufficient results.

### AX – Google’s Open Agentic Orchestrator - (Hacker News)

https://agentexecutor.io

# AX Summary

AX is an open-source platform designed specifically for running agentic AI workloads at scale. Built on Google's Agent Substrate research, it provides four core primitives—Tasks (isolated execution), Workspaces (environment setup), Gateways (network policies), and Models (configuration management)—that allow developers to declare and execute agent tasks declaratively. The platform can scale to billions of concurrent tasks per cluster with sub-second suspension and resumption capabilities, enabling efficient resource utilization by multiplexing idle agents across shared worker resources.

Unlike traditional orchestrators built for microservices or batch jobs, AX is purpose-built for stateful, long-running agents that alternate between intensive computation and waiting for model/tool responses. It includes generative features (like natural language workspace setup), supports diverse workloads from interactive coding to headless testing, and emphasizes developer ergonomics for both application builders and AI researchers. The platform is production-ready and available as an open-source Apache 2.0 licensed project on GitHub.

### Samsung is expected to more than double output of its HBM4 and HBM4E DRAM - (Hacker News)

https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/09/20/samsung-to-double-hbm4-output-next-year-sources-say

# Summary

Samsung Electronics plans to more than double its HBM4 high-bandwidth memory chip output next year, with total production expected to reach approximately 250,000 wafers monthly from around 180,000 currently. The company is significantly ramping up outsourced glass carrier cleaning—essential materials for HBM production—from 20,000 sheets per month this year to 50,000 sheets next year, a 2.5-fold increase that signals the substantial production expansion.

The expansion focuses on sixth-generation HBM4 and seventh-generation HBM4E chips with 12-layer stacks and higher, which are projected to comprise about 80% of Samsung's total HBM output next year, up from roughly 40% currently. This shift reflects Samsung's strategy to center production on high-value HBM4 products, capitalizing on growing demand from major customers like Nvidia as the AI chip market accelerates.
