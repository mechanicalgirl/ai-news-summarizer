### Production-grade LLMs and agents: a field guide - (Stack Overflow Blog)

https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/10/19/production-grade-llms-and-agents-a-field-guide/

# Summary

This Stack Overflow article presents a maturity model for transitioning LLM agents from impressive demos to production-ready systems. The core insight is that production reliability comes not from larger or more deterministic models, but from wrapping contained AI decisions in well-engineered, testable deterministic machinery—containment rather than trust. The framework outlines six levels: Notebook (basic demo), Determinism (bounded agent actions), Evaluation (automated testing gates), Confidence (calibrated decision-making), Safety & Governance (layered guardrails and audit trails), and Operability (observability and cost control).

The article emphasizes addressing weaknesses sequentially—most teams skip early foundational steps like determinism and evaluation, then struggle with higher-level concerns. It includes a quick self-assessment checklist and links to 25 deep-dive articles covering topics from structured outputs and confidence scoring to PII handling, audit logging, and infrastructure requirements. The key takeaway: production systems succeed by containing the model's scope to minimal decisions and surrounding it with observable, testable, auditable systems rather than relying on model improvements alone.

### Eight Bits of Trust; eMudhra's CA keys cracked in milliseconds - (Hacker News)

https://scotthelme.co.uk/eight-bits-of-trust-emudhras-ca-keys-cracked-in-milliseconds/

# Summary

eMudhra, a certificate authority, generated eight intermediate certificates with critically weak private keys in April 2025—some as simple as the numbers 4, 56, and 115 instead of the required 384-bit random numbers. These keys could be cracked in milliseconds on standard hardware, meaning anyone viewing the public certificates could recover the private keys and forge SSL/TLS certificates for any website, as well as fraudulent code-signing certificates. While mainstream browsers like Chrome and Firefox quickly blocked these intermediates and enforce Certificate Transparency protections, the vulnerability exposes a massive gap in non-browser TLS clients.

The real danger lies in the broader software ecosystem: curl installers, CI/CD pipelines, IoT devices, package managers, and other tools typically don't check Certificate Transparency or revocation and still trust the compromised eMudhra root. This means attackers could impersonate high-value targets like payment processors and manipulate sensitive server-to-server communications, firmware updates, and software installations across millions of devices worldwide—many with frozen root stores that won't receive updates until 2039. The author successfully demonstrated forging certificates for api.stripe.com that were accepted as valid by default configurations across Linux, macOS, and Windows systems.

### Whatever Happened to Fire Escapes - (Hacker News)

https://practical.engineering/blog/2026/10/20/whatever-happened-to-fire-escapes

# Summary

Fire escapes emerged in mid-1800s America as a practical solution to evacuate tall tenement buildings during fires, becoming mandatory after a deadly 1860 New York bakery fire. However, their effectiveness was always debated—the Franklin Institute questioned their value as early as 1881, and the 1911 Triangle Shirtwaist Factory fire tragically demonstrated that fire escapes could actually harm evacuees when they buckled under weight. This incident sparked decades of controversy between those who believed external fire escapes should be prohibited in favor of better internal stairwells, and pragmatists who argued for improving existing external designs.

By the mid-20th century, fire escapes had accumulated numerous disadvantages: they cluttered building facades, created security vulnerabilities, required extensive maintenance, and didn't solve the core problem of safe evacuation. Building codes ultimately shifted away from mandating external fire escapes as better alternatives for fire safety became available, contributing to their gradual disappearance from modern buildings—though they remain fixtures on older city skylines, representing a safety solution that never fully lived up to its promise.

### The 1.5°C goal of the Paris Agreement is no longer viable - (Hacker News)

https://unric.org/en/unep-emissions-gap-report-2026-1-5-degree-overshoot/

# Summary

The UN Environment Programme's 2026 Emissions Gap Report concludes that the Paris Agreement's target of limiting global warming to 1.5°C is now unachievable, with overshoot inevitable within the coming decade. The report shifts focus to a new goal: keeping warming below 2°C while working to return to 1.5°C by century's end. This requires dramatic emissions reductions of 18% by 2030 and 69% by 2040 relative to 2025 levels—far exceeding current government commitments, which fall short by nearly 90%.

To close this "leadership gap," the UN calls for urgent action across multiple sectors: tripling renewable energy capacity by 2035, phasing out fossil fuels with specific timelines, halting deforestation, and removing 320 gigatons of CO2 from the atmosphere by 2100. Despite progress since 2015—including rapid growth in solar energy and reduced coal/oil growth—efforts remain insufficient, particularly regarding fossil fuels. The report emphasizes that reversing the trend is still possible but requires immediate, coordinated global action across all economic sectors.

### Show HN: SimCity, but with Permits - (Hacker News)

https://permit.petercai.com/

# Permit Pending Summary

**Permit Pending** is a free, browser-based city simulation that puts you in the role of an elected mayor rather than an all-powerful god. Unlike traditional city-builders, every development project—housing, transit, industry—must navigate realistic bureaucratic hurdles including public hearings, zoning board reviews, neighbor objections, lawsuits, and appeals. The game emphasizes political strategy alongside economic management, forcing players to balance competing interests: new housing lowers rents but angers homeowners; new industry creates jobs but pollution; and improved transit attracts development that sparks opposition.

The game features a living economy where residents have ages and incomes, businesses hire and relocate, and all systems interconnect dynamically. Players face re-election every four years and must manage political capital wisely while understanding voter demographics and sentiment. With no win condition, the sandbox gameplay challenges you to build your ideal city while staying in office long enough to achieve it—a task complicated by the fact that every solution creates new angry voters. The game runs entirely in browsers without requiring downloads or accounts.
