### Payments Canada announces launch of Canada's Real-Time Rail (RTR) payment system - (Hacker News)

https://www.payments.ca/canada-launches-real-time-rail-bringing-247-payment-infrastructure-national-economy

# Summary

Canada has launched the Real-Time Rail (RTR), a new 24/7 national payment system that enables instant clearing and settlement of payments among participating financial institutions, credit unions, and payment service providers. The system, owned and operated by Payments Canada, is projected to generate up to $14.5 billion in cost savings over the next decade through reduced processing costs, lower cash and cheque usage, and eliminated payment delays, with an additional $16 billion in economic gains expected as new features are added.

The RTR distinguishes itself as the first real-time payment system among G7 countries to launch with mandatory centralized fraud safeguards, including confirmation of payee and fraud scoring mechanisms. Initially featuring a small cohort of participants including fintech companies like Wealthsimple and Questrade, the system is designed to rapidly scale, with all major Canadian banks expected to join within the first year, making real-time payments accessible to over 90% of Canadians.

https://www.foldableflight.com

### The same H100 costs $2.69 or $12.29 an hour depending where you rent it - (Hacker News)

https://github.com/datareceipts/data-receipts/tree/main/109-gpu-pricing

# Summary

This GitHub repository analyzes H100 GPU rental pricing across major cloud providers (AWS, Azure) and alternative cloud vendors (RunPod, etc.), normalizing costs to dollars per GPU per hour. The analysis reveals a stark 4.57× price difference between the cheapest option (RunPod Community at $2.69/GPU-hour) and the most expensive (Azure at $12.29/GPU-hour) for identical H100 SXM 80GB chips—meaning a month of continuous GPU rental ranges from $1,964 to $8,972 depending solely on the provider.

The researchers emphasize this spread is not pure profit margin, as cheaper options sacrifice critical features like high-speed InfiniBand interconnects needed for multi-node training, enterprise SLAs, and compliance guarantees that justify hyperscalers' higher prices. The repository provides Python scripts to collect and normalize pricing data, with limitations noted including coverage of only on-demand pricing (excluding cheaper reserved/spot rates) and single-region comparisons.

### I Saw Your HN Comment - (Hacker News)

https://joshstrange.com/2026/11/09/i-saw-your-hn-comment/

# Summary

Josh Strange laments how cold emails triggered by his online posts and Hacker News comments have deteriorated from genuine connections to mostly deceptive sales pitches. He notes that LLMs have made it easier for marketers to craft personalized, seemingly authentic emails that pose fake questions to build rapport before revealing their real pitch—a tactic he finds dishonest and time-wasting, since he typically responds thoughtfully to genuine inquiries only to be manipulated into considering products or services.

While Strange acknowledges that cold outreach is inevitable and he's done it himself, he takes issue with the deceptive framing and the erosion of trust it creates. He now approaches every email with suspicion, responding minimally at first to screen for hidden pitches, which saddens him because it transforms what was once a source of serendipity and interesting connections into a defensive chore. Though he refuses to hide his email address, he remains frustrated by how online discourse has become material for sales manipulation.

### Magenta solar panels may be agrivoltaics' cleverest trick yet - (Hacker News)

https://www.anthropocenemagazine.org/2026/10/magenta-solar-panels-may-be-agrivoltaics-cleverest-trick-yet/

# Summary

Researchers are developing semi-transparent magenta solar panels that could revolutionize agrivoltaics—the practice of combining crop farming with solar energy production on the same land. These panels selectively capture certain light wavelengths to generate electricity while allowing the red and blue wavelengths essential for photosynthesis to pass through to plants below. A new study found that broccoli grown under these magenta-tinted panels produced comparable yields to unshaded plants, despite taking 25 days longer to mature, while the panels also improved growing conditions by reducing water stress.

This innovation could address land-use conflicts by allowing farmers to generate both food and renewable energy from the same space. The researchers estimate that dedicating just one percent of U.S. farmland to agrivoltaics could meet up to 20 percent of the nation's electricity needs. However, the study was preliminary, and researchers acknowledge that larger-scale, multi-season tests across different climates will be necessary before agrivoltaics becomes a mainstream farming practice.

### Zebkit – Chromium as an embeddable library - (Hacker News)

https://github.com/ikey4u/zebkit

# Summary

Zebkit is a lightweight, cross-platform browser engine library that embeds Chromium's core components (Blink, V8, PDFium, FFmpeg) directly into applications without requiring Electron or a separate browser runtime. Developers can integrate it into C, C++, Rust, Python, Go, or JavaScript applications while maintaining their own windows, toolkit, and distribution methods. It offers a unified API across Linux, macOS, and Windows, with 34% smaller footprint and faster startup times compared to Electron.

Beyond web rendering, zebkit provides comprehensive APIs for PDF manipulation, media handling, 2D drawing, native UI dialogs, and browser automation through Playwright's API running natively at 3-10x faster than over CDP. It includes built-in access to 17 Chromium libraries (SQLite, OpenSSL, FFmpeg, etc.), security-by-default sandboxing, desktop integration features, and support for AI agents through MCP tools—making it a modular alternative to Electron for applications that need web capabilities without building entirely within a browser runtime.

### What is malloc doing, anyway? - (Hacker News)

https://benmandrew.com/articles/what-is-malloc-doing-anyway

# Summary

This article explains how `malloc` and `free` work under the hood. Rather than making expensive system calls for each memory allocation, `malloc` requests large blocks from the kernel upfront and then manages smaller allocations internally from that pool. The article demonstrates this by showing that allocating 1,000 small blocks only triggers two kernel `brk` syscalls. It then explains the process address space layout and illustrates why a simple "bump allocator" that calls the kernel for each request is inefficient (22.7x slower than real `malloc`).

The article then develops a toy allocator that addresses these inefficiencies by implementing chunks with size headers, free lists for memory reuse, and mechanisms for splitting large chunks and coalescing adjacent free chunks back together. This approach mirrors how real allocators like glibc work, allowing efficient reuse of freed memory while minimizing expensive kernel interactions. The detailed examples show how chunks are allocated, freed, merged, and reused throughout the heap's lifecycle.
