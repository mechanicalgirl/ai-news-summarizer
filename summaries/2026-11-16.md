### The Secret ThinkPad Powerbutton Code to Bring Dead Laptops Back to Life (2007) - (Hacker News)

https://masnick.com/posts/the-secret-thinkpad-powerbutton-code-to-bring-dead-laptops-back-to-life/

# Summary

Mike Masnick's brand new Lenovo ThinkPad Z61t suddenly died completely—no lights, no response to the power button, and excessive heat despite being in sleep mode. When he called IBM support, the technician provided an unusual "power button code" to revive it: unplug the AC adapter and remove the battery, press the power button 10 times at one-second intervals, hold it for 30 seconds, reinstall the battery, and press the power button again. The technician explained it was meant to discharge static electricity that had caused a short circuit. Remarkably, the laptop came back to life.

The article has since become legendary among ThinkPad users, with hundreds of comments from people confirming the trick worked for their dead laptops across various ThinkPad models (T42, T60, T61, X41, R61, etc.). While most report success, a few users note it didn't work for them, though some found alternative solutions like reseating RAM. The post has become a go-to resource for anyone with a mysteriously dead ThinkPad, and commenters credit it with saving them hundreds of dollars and preventing data loss.

### Many of Amazon’s Black Friday and Cyber Monday “Deals” Offered No Savings - (Hacker News)

http://groundworkcollaborative.org/news/amazons-decoy-deals-groundwork-investigation-reveals-nearly-1-in-3-black-friday-and-cyber-monday-deals-advertised-by-amazon-offered-no-savings-to-shoppers-nearly-1-in-10-w/

# Summary

A Groundwork Collaborative investigation found that Amazon's Black Friday and Cyber Monday sales are largely deceptive, with nearly one-third of advertised "deals" offering no savings compared to prices from the previous month, and approximately one in ten products actually costing more during the sale events. The analysis examined over 350 products from Amazon's deal pages across 2024 and 2025, revealing instances where items were marked up significantly—such as Hunter rain boots priced 59% higher and UGG boots 83% higher than their pre-sale prices.

The report calls for federal and state regulators to implement stricter oversight of misleading discounts and require retailers to display the lowest price from the previous 30 days when advertising sales. This investigation continues Groundwork's broader work on corporate pricing abuses and adds pressure on major retailers during a period when millions of Americans rely on holiday sales to manage rising costs.

### Universal XSS in uBlock Origin from website-driven element picker - (Hacker News)

https://iter.ca/post/ubo-uxss/

# Summary

A researcher discovered a critical universal XSS vulnerability in uBlock Origin's element picker feature that allowed malicious websites to inject arbitrary JavaScript code into any webpage. The attack exploited three main weaknesses: first, the attacker could extract the extension's random UUID by hooking XHR requests made by uBlock's default filters; second, they could then iframe the invisible element picker and send crafted messages to it; and third, they could inject newline characters into filter parameters to create malicious "trusted filters" that execute JavaScript on any domain.

The vulnerability was particularly dangerous because it required only a single user click on a malicious website to compromise security on all other sites, and the injected filters would persist until the user manually recompiled their filters. The issue has since been patched by implementing a secret password verification system between the content script and element picker to prevent unauthorized message communications.

### Interpreting claims that a theorem is correct because it was checked in Lean - (Hacker News)

https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2026/11/12/and-the-proof-is-checked-in-lean/

# Summary

This article explains how to properly evaluate claims that mathematical theorems are correct because their proofs have been verified in Lean, particularly for AI-generated code. The author outlines four critical questions that must be answered before accepting such claims: (1) Does the Lean statement actually match the informal theorem statement in the paper? (2) Does the code compile successfully? (3) Does the code prove the stated theorem without hidden tricks or redefinitions? and (4) Does the code exploit any bugs in Lean's kernel? The author emphasizes that many AI-generated theorem dumps, including OpenAI's recent 722-paper collection, have not been systematically tested against these criteria, though tools like "lake comparator" exist to verify the integrity of proofs.

The key insight is that Lean verification is a valuable signal of correctness, but requires careful validation, especially with untrusted AI-generated code. The author notes that statements and proofs must be distinguished, that some formalizations may rely on thousands of lines of custom definitions not yet verified by humans, and that multiple independent Lean kernels can be used to rule out exploitation of kernel bugs. Without addressing all four questions, claims about Lean-verified theorem correctness should be treated cautiously.

### OOS – an open operating system for the Rigol Scopes - (Hacker News)

https://www.eevblog.com/forum/testgear/oos-an-open-operating-system-for-the-rigol-scopes/

# Summary

OOS is an open-source operating system replacement for Rigol oscilloscopes (currently DHO800/DHO900 series) that runs alongside the stock system on the device's SD card. Built with a Linux 6.12 kernel and a Rust-based scope application, it offers extensive features including advanced acquisition and triggering modes, 35 automatic measurements, 25 protocol decoders, spectrum analysis, eye diagrams, jitter analysis, and remote control via Ethernet or WLAN.

The project is experimental and currently only fully tested on the DHO924S model, though it has shown compatibility with other DHO8xx/DHO9xx variants. Installation can be done via USB stick or a second SD card, with the ability to easily revert to the stock system. The developer is seeking feedback from users on other models and welcomes bug reports and feature requests on GitHub, emphasizing that measurements made with OOS are not calibrated to any standard and should be used at the user's own risk.

### The Active Directory Era Is Finally Over - (Hacker News)

https://infisical.com/blog/active-directory-machine-identity

# Summary

The article argues that Active Directory (AD), while effective for its era, is fundamentally unsuited for managing identities in modern cloud infrastructure. Originally designed for human approval of long-lived physical servers, AD cannot scale to handle today's ephemeral cloud resources, automated deployments, and distributed architectures. The core problem is that AD's unified directory approach breaks down when machines self-serve their identities through Kubernetes pods, cloud provider metadata services, and CI/CD pipelines—making it impossible to maintain accurate visibility and proper authorization.

To address this gap, organizations are shifting toward two modern approaches: identity federation (where platforms trust each other's authentication through OIDC standards) and secrets management brokers that centrally store and distribute credentials to workloads at runtime. These solutions acknowledge that the cloud era requires smaller security perimeters, self-service machine identity, and continuous authentication rather than AD's monolithic directory model. While neither approach is as simple as AD's historical unified approach, they're necessary to properly manage the complexity of modern machine identity.

### Researchers track whole body movement with just a smartwatch - (Hacker News)

https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/11/researchers-track-whole-body-movement-just-smartwatch

# Summary

Cornell researchers have developed EchoMotion, a new technology that tracks a person's entire body movement using only an off-the-shelf smartwatch equipped with ultrasonic sound waves and a lightweight AI model. The system uses the smartwatch's built-in speaker and microphone to emit inaudible sound waves and detect echoes bouncing off the user's limbs, translating these patterns into precise body position data. When tested on an unmodified Samsung Galaxy Watch 6, the system achieved joint-tracking accuracy within four inches without calibration, improving to 2.4 inches with a brief 12-minute calibration session.

The key advantages of EchoMotion over existing body-tracking systems include enhanced privacy (no cameras needed), significantly lower battery consumption (40 times less than wearable cameras), and compatibility with commercial smartwatches without modification. The technology has potential applications in fitness tracking, form correction during exercise, and virtual reality systems. Researchers are now developing it for real-world use and exploring integration with smartglasses for comprehensive activity monitoring.
