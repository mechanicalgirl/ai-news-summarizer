### Pinterest and Intel Take Open-Source Profiling to Internet Scale - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/pinterest-intel-profiling/

# Summary

Pinterest and Intel have partnered to extend gProfiler, an open-source continuous profiler, to handle profiling at internet scale across Pinterest's massive infrastructure of hundreds of thousands of instances. Rather than building a proprietary solution, they contributed enhancements back to the open-source project, adding capabilities like dynamic profiling, hardware-level telemetry via Intel PerfSpect, wall-time profiling, and performance-counter flame graphs. The unified tool consolidates fragmented profiling from multiple languages (Java, Python, C++, Go, Node.js) into correlated flame graphs without requiring code changes.

The enhanced gProfiler operates as an efficient orchestration layer that discovers processes, coordinates language-specific profilers, and merges their data for comprehensive performance visibility. Agents remain lightweight when dormant (less than 1% CPU, 100 MB memory) and can be remotely configured through a UI or API. The architecture separates collection on individual hosts from analysis and visualization in gProfiler Performance Studio, which stores profiles in S3 and ClickHouse for analytical querying, enabling engineers to compare performance across services, hosts, and time periods without manual correlation.

### Article: LLM Reliability Layers Under Test: Accuracy, Yield, and the Limits of Verification - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/articles/llm-reliability-layers-limits-accuracy/

# Summary

This article examines the effectiveness of reliability verification layers added to large language models (LLMs) to improve accuracy on clinical information extraction tasks. The researchers tested six different verification approaches—from deterministic checks to human review—on the CADEC corpus (a dataset of adverse drug reactions). Contrary to their hypothesis that adding more layers would incrementally improve accuracy, they found that these layers primarily sorted answers by trustworthiness but corrected very few errors, with accuracy remaining disappointingly low across configurations.

Key findings revealed important limitations of LLM verification: deterministic code can only prove answers wrong, not right; recognition tasks (selecting from existing options) vastly outperformed generation tasks (inventing answers), with 10x higher accuracy; and most critically, no verification layer could recover errors that the initial extraction phase missed. The study demonstrates that while reliability layers can help manage LLM outputs, they cannot fundamentally overcome the model's inherent limitations—real accuracy gains come from better initial extraction or matching domain conventions, not from adding verification checks alone.

### Cloudflare Develops Public Certificate Authority for Post-Quantum Web Security - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/cloudflare-merkle-certificates/

# Summary

Cloudflare has developed Merkle Tree Certificates (MTCs) as an alternative to traditional certificate chains to address post-quantum cryptography challenges. In a Chrome experiment, MTCs demonstrated 9% faster median performance by batching certificate data into append-only Merkle trees and allowing clients to verify certificates with compact inclusion proofs instead of transmitting full certificate chains. The approach was tested on billions of certificates across 50% of Chrome Beta 146 users.

Building on this technology, Cloudflare is developing a public certificate authority to support MTC issuance, targeting inclusion in Chrome's quantum-resistant Root Store in early 2027. This initiative addresses a critical scaling challenge: post-quantum signatures are approximately 40 times larger than classical signatures, significantly increasing bandwidth and storage requirements. The solution uses landmark-based verification and independent transparency log cosigners to enable secure, scalable certificate management at Internet scale, though production deployment and broader ecosystem support remain necessary for widespread adoption.

### AWS Launches TorchNeuron to Run PyTorch Models on AWS Trainium Without Modifications - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/aws-torchneuron-trainium-pytorch/

# Summary

AWS has launched TorchNeuron, a native PyTorch backend that allows developers to run existing PyTorch models on AWS Trainium AI chips with minimal code changes—typically requiring only a single configuration modification to switch hardware. This eliminates the need to rewrite and revalidate models when migrating to new chips, addressing a major pain point since 85% of AI researchers use PyTorch. TorchNeuron supports distributed training and various PyTorch parallelism APIs, and Amazon validated it against 100,000+ popular models and 800+ ML architectures.

Beyond reducing engineering effort, TorchNeuron enables enterprises to share hardware capacity between training and inference workloads and offers developers access to custom operators through the Neuron Kernel Interface for performance optimization. The announcement follows Google's similar release of TorchTPU and has already gained adoption from organizations like Cartesia and the Lieber Institute, though experts note the true test will be achieving competitive performance and cost efficiency in production environments.

This represents a significant advancement in making expensive GPU resources more efficiently utilized in distributed Kubernetes environments.

### Article: Agentic AI Architecture: Current Challenges and Future Opportunities - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/articles/agentic-ai-architecture-challenges-opportunities/

# Summary

Agentic AI architecture represents an emerging software paradigm that extends traditional microservices and cloud-native architectures by integrating AI agents capable of working with context, memory, and specialized protocols like MCP (Model Context Protocol) and A2A (Agent-to-Agent). This new architecture shifts software systems from purely deterministic, data-processing operations to knowledge-level support, enabling AI agents to handle tasks that traditionally required human judgment and decision-making. However, the adoption of agentic AI brings significant complexity, requiring architects and engineers to rethink established practices in areas such as security, observability, and system coordination.

The article emphasizes that agentic AI adoption will be gradual, with these systems co-existing alongside traditional architectures for the foreseeable future until they eventually merge into a unified paradigm. Key challenges include managing bounded-context integration, implementing task-dependent coordination strategies, addressing new security risks, and establishing proper observability. To support this transition, platforms and vendors are expected to emerge to absorb common components and simplify the delivery of complex agentic AI systems, making adoption more accessible to organizations navigating this significant architectural shift.

### Pinterest Replaces Journey Clustering with 4B Qwen3 LLM, Reports Improved Engagement - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/pinterest-journey/

# Summary

Pinterest replaced its multi-stage clustering pipeline for inferring user journeys with a fine-tuned 4-billion-parameter Qwen3 language model, achieving a 1.1% improvement in email click-through rates and 1.3% higher push notification opens. The new system treats journey inference as a generative task, analyzing up to 150 user actions over 360 days to identify coherent, longer-term goals (like a kitchen renovation project) rather than fragmented interests. This approach better captures the connection between related activities across multiple sessions while suppressing noise and one-off engagement.

To train the model without extensive manual labeling, Pinterest used frontier LLMs to generate synthetic training data from user activity histories. The company selected a 4B parameter model after testing 0.6B to 8B variants, balancing quality and serving efficiency. The infrastructure uses NVIDIA Dynamo and vLLM with GPU acceleration, continuous batching, and PagedAttention optimization, though significant throughput gains also came from skipping inference for users processed within the preceding two days.

### Tokio Releases Topcoat: A Batteries-Included Full-Stack Rust Framework - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/topcoat-rust-framework/

# Summary

Tokio has released Topcoat, a new batteries-included full-stack Rust web framework that has moved into public development with a v0.9 release. Topcoat distinguishes itself by staying entirely server-rendered while adding reactivity through cross-compiled Rust expressions (via `$(...)` syntax) that run on both server and browser without requiring WebAssembly or separate client builds. The framework includes integrated features like a `view!` macro for HTML, file-tree-based routing, asset pipelines, Tailwind support without Node.js, and the Toasty async ORM, positioning it closer to HTMX and Phoenix LiveView than to React-style frameworks.

The framework targets mainstream database-driven web applications rather than complex, large-scale systems, filling a gap in the Rust ecosystem for Django/Rails-style productivity. It includes a vendored component library called Topcoat UI inspired by shadcn/ui, allowing teams to customize components directly. While community reception has been enthusiastic about having a full-stack Rust framework with integrated type safety, some note the project is still in early development (0.x) and lacks features like authentication and admin panels that mature frameworks offer, though these are on the roadmap.

### Entire's Git Forge Rethinks Git Collaboration for Coding Agents - (InfoQ)

https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/entire-git-forge-coding-agents/

# Summary

Entire, founded by former GitHub CEO Thomas Dohmke, is a new Git forge designed specifically for AI-human developer collaboration. Unlike traditional platforms like GitHub that optimize for human-to-human asynchronous review, Entire centers around orchestrating, versioning, and auditing AI agents working alongside humans. Built on a distributed architecture offering 89x faster pushes than GitHub, its core innovation is "trails"—a system that displays evaluation scores updated on every code push, helping developers assess whether a PR is stable or needs further iteration by AI agents.

The platform introduces two key concepts to support effective AI-agent collaboration: evaluation agents that score code changes based on quality, security, and review requirements, triggering new iterations when needed, and "continuous memory" that preserves accumulated repository knowledge so agents can maintain context across sessions rather than starting from scratch. This is implemented through open-source tools that map the codebase and create a persistent memory layer combining code history with previous agent sessions, ensuring agents always have proper context for their work.

### Human-Pilled - (Radar)

https://www.oreilly.com/radar/human-pilled/

# Summary

This article by Tim O'Reilly explores the tension between "The Bitter Lesson"—Richard Sutton's observation that raw computational power ultimately outperforms human expertise in AI—and what O'Reilly calls being "human-pilled," or believing humans still have essential value in an AI-driven economy. While acknowledging that brute-force computation will win out, O'Reilly argues that the AI industry fails to adequately address how to create economic opportunities for humans in this future, focusing instead on safety concerns and job loss predictions rather than designing mechanisms to harness human expertise.

O'Reilly contends that human value lies not in judgment or taste alone, but in the unique ability to direct AI's vast possibilities through conscious attention and context. He illustrates this with an example of requesting an AI perspective rather than a draft, showing how human choice and framing shape AI outputs in ways that reflect individual experience and understanding. Rather than viewing humans and AI as in direct competition, O'Reilly suggests we need to develop new markets and architectures that leverage human expertise as a complementary force alongside AI capabilities.

### Why AI Agents Are Repeating 2019’s Microservices Mistake - (Radar)

https://www.oreilly.com/radar/why-ai-agents-are-repeating-2019s-microservices-mistake/

# Summary

The article draws a parallel between the architectural mistakes made during microservices adoption in 2019 and the current trajectory of AI agent development. Just as breaking monolithic applications into microservices improved scalability but moved complexity into the gaps between services—making system behavior harder to observe—AI agents are following the same pattern. When workflows are split across multiple agents, individual components may function correctly while overall output quality gradually degrades. The author illustrates this with a personal example where three agents working as designed still produced incorrect results because one agent dropped a field that proved critical downstream.

The critical difference is speed and scale: AI agents are far cheaper and faster to create than microservices, making it easier to repeat these architectural mistakes at much larger scale. The 2019 microservices crisis manifested as slower delivery schedules when teams struggled to manage hundreds of interconnected services. AI agents are already heading toward the same coordination and observability problems, but potentially affecting far more systems. Research on multi-agent frameworks has identified 14 recurring failure modes, most stemming from poor system specification and agent coordination rather than model deficiencies—suggesting the solution requires architectural discipline, not just better AI models.
