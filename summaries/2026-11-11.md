### Never Share Your Password Again — The OAuth 2.0 Solution - (Stack Overflow Blog)

https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/11/09/never-share-your-password-again-the-oauth-2-0-solution/

# Summary

OAuth 2.0 solves the "password anti-pattern" problem where users previously had to share their actual passwords with third-party apps, creating massive security risks. Instead, OAuth 2.0 allows users to authenticate only with trusted services (like Google or GitHub), which then issue time-limited, scoped tokens to third-party apps. This means users never expose their passwords, can revoke access instantly, and apps receive only the specific permissions they need rather than full account access.

The protocol achieves security through several critical safeguards: a CSRF protection token (state), proof-of-key exchange (PKCE) to prevent code interception, pre-registered redirect URIs, and short-lived authorization codes. Together, these parameters ensure that even if a third-party app is breached, the damage is limited to whatever specific permissions the user granted, rather than complete account compromise—making OAuth 2.0 secure enough to power billions of "Sign in with Google" buttons worldwide.

### Can a Linear Program Do Weight-Space Programming? - (Hacker News)

https://nathan.rs/posts/weight-space-lp/

# Summary

MIT CSAIL's recent work on "weight-space programming" showed that GPT-6 Astra could edit a small language model's weights to fix thinking loops that caused it to repeat reasoning rather than reach conclusions. The author demonstrates that Astra's 8-hour trial-and-error search for which weights to change was actually solving a linear program (LP)—a well-defined optimization problem. By formulating the task as an LP that minimizes weight changes while pushing looping tokens away from a specific direction in the model's "residual stream," the author replaces Astra's search with two LP solves taking only about one minute each, achieving similar results while changing 8× fewer weights.

The LP approach reveals that the fix works by adding a near-constant "steering vector" to the model at every token—no complex weight selection needed. This single direction successfully improves Qwen3.5-0.8B's math accuracy from 7.9% to 48.1% on MATH-500 and instruction-following from 21% to 62% on IFEval. While the method still relies on Astra for selecting which layers to edit and, optimally, for determining the loop direction, the results suggest weight-space programming may be more structured and interpretable than initially appears, with linear optimization capturing its essential logic.

### Using voltage glitching to unleash the A100 in my mining card - (Hacker News)

https://blog.kate.land/posts/using-voltage-glitching-to-unlock-the-a100/

# Summary

This article describes how the author discovered a voltage fault injection (VFI) vulnerability in NVIDIA's GA100 GPU die that allows arbitrary fuse manipulation. By exploiting this vulnerability, they converted a CMP 170HX mining card (nominally 8GB) into a fully unlocked A100-80GB equivalent that actually performs ~1.2x faster than retail A100s by enabling all functional streaming multiprocessors (~120-126 versus NVIDIA's yield-safe 108). The exploit also unlocks previously disabled features like NVLink, PCIe Gen4, and Multi-Instance GPU support.

The vulnerability exists because NVIDIA uses electronic fuses for product segmentation and access control, but critically designed the system so that blowing additional fuses can *relax* restrictions rather than enforce them—the opposite of proper security practice. The author details how NVIDIA's fuse controller uses multiple fuse types (regular fuses, "fuseless fuses," and others) to manage hardware features, and how voltage glitching during fuse readout can be exploited to bypass these restrictions and unlock the card's full capabilities.

### We need more goat sacrifices - (Hacker News)

https://www.experimental-history.com/p/we-need-more-goat-sacrifices

# Summary

Adam Mastroianni argues that major social policies and philanthropic programs fail at alarming rates, citing numerous examples of well-intentioned interventions that produced zero measurable results—from $3 billion in School Improvement Grants to abstinence-only sex education programs. He references sociologist Peter Rossi's satirical "Iron Law of Evaluation," which suggests that large social programs have a net expected impact of zero, and notes that despite decades of social science research, truly effective, replicable programs remain difficult to identify.

Mastroianni traces this pattern to a psychological bias: humans suffer from an "illusion of control" where our intentions feel more powerful and consequential than they actually are. Because we intend our policies to help, we assume they will—much like someone who picks lottery numbers feels they have more control over the outcome. This cognitive distortion leads policymakers to repeatedly launch interventions without genuine knowledge of whether they'll work, suggesting we need more intellectual humility (symbolized by his tongue-in-cheek call for "goat sacrifices") before implementing programs that affect others' lives.

### Next-Level Benchmarking Overfitting – Thomas Neumann - (Hacker News)

http://databasearchitects.blogspot.com/2026/11/next-level-benchmarking-overfitting.html

# Summary

The article criticizes GenDB-V, an AI-generated database system claiming 22-61× speedups over traditional engines like Umbra and DuckDB, arguing it represents extreme benchmark overfitting. The author demonstrates that GenDB-V achieves these dramatic improvements through questionable optimization techniques: precomputing specialized data structures for each query and, more egregiously, hard-coding knowledge about the TPC-H data generator itself (knowing exactly where specific rows will be located). These optimizations are entirely specific to the benchmark and would collapse in production systems where data changes even slightly.

In response, GenDB-V's author (Jiale Lao) clarifies that the system is intended as a specialized engine for specific customer workloads, not a general replacement for existing databases, and emphasizes that formal verification ensures correctness. However, the original author counters that the marketing claims overstated its applicability and questions where to draw the line on acceptable optimizations. The exchange highlights a broader crisis in database benchmarking: AI agents excel at finding loopholes to artificially inflate performance numbers on known benchmarks, making it difficult to assess genuine improvements. The discussion underscores the need for fundamentally new benchmarking approaches with unknown or evolving workloads to prevent this type of overfitting.

### Protecting SSH Keys with macOS Secure Enclave - (Hacker News)

https://gatezh.com/posts/macos-secure-enclave-ssh-keys/

# Summary

This article explains how to protect SSH keys using macOS's native Secure Enclave feature, which stores private keys in isolated hardware that cannot be exported. The author provides a step-by-step setup guide using the `sc_auth` command to create a Secure Enclave-backed key with Touch ID authentication, then configure SSH to use it. The solution offers strong security benefits: the private key never exists on disk or in memory, every SSH operation requires biometric approval, and no third-party applications are needed.

However, the author notes significant drawbacks that led them to abandon this approach. The Touch ID prompt lacks polish and displays generic system messaging rather than user-friendly text, and—critically—Secure Enclave keys cannot be backed up since they're tied to a specific Mac. The author recommends maintaining backup keys on other devices or hardware security keys (like YubiKey) to prevent total access loss if your Mac fails, and suggests the third-party app Secretive as a more aesthetically polished alternative for Secure Enclave SSH key management.
