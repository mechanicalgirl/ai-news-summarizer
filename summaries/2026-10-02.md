### Building an Ultra-High Throughput AI-SQL Engine - (Python Weekly)

https://fsdatalab.github.io/blog/introducing-quail/

# Summary

This article introduces Quail, a query-aware inference layer designed to optimize AI-SQL queries by jointly optimizing query planning and model inference for open-weight LLMs. Rather than relying on expensive closed-source APIs, Quail addresses the inefficiency of using general-purpose inference engines like vLLM for database-scale AI-SQL workloads. The authors demonstrate that vLLM suffers from significant overhead issues—including CPU scheduling bottlenecks and "KV regret" (discarding key-value cache data needed later)—resulting in performance 27.55x slower than theoretical optimal on their test query.

Quail achieves substantial performance improvements by allowing query plans to control LLM inference, delivering an average of 1.84x speedup over optimized vLLM baselines and up to 14x faster on specific queries. The system enables users to run AI-enhanced SQL queries with reduced computational costs, as demonstrated through practical examples like filtering 100,000 movie reviews. This approach makes AI-SQL more accessible and efficient for database-scale workloads while maintaining the ability to handle millions of model inference calls within a single query.

### Deser: Rethinking Rust Serialization - (Armin Ronacher's Thoughts and Writings)

https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/9/29/deser/

# Summary

Armin Ronacher discusses the limitations of Serde, Rust's dominant serialization library, and introduces Deser as an alternative. He highlights three specific corner cases where Serde's design creates problems: internally tagged enums breaking with arbitrary precision numbers, flattening corrupting integer keys, and custom deserializers not composing with wrapper types like Option. These issues stem from Serde's fundamental architecture rather than bugs, and are protected by its stability guarantees, making them difficult to fix.

Deser reimagines serialization by inverting Serde's architecture—instead of types driving deserialization recursively, formats push events into a sink that keeps state on the heap. This approach avoids stack overflows, enables suspendable deserialization, and provides a more extensible data model without relying on in-band signalling tricks. While Deser likely cannot replace Serde due to ecosystem entrenchment, it aims to be a viable alternative by supporting all major self-describing formats (JSON, YAML, TOML, XML, etc.) with comparable performance tradeoffs, offering users options for scenarios where Serde's design limitations are problematic.
