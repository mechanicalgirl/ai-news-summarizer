### Desert Ant Labs: local, fast models that run on device - (Hacker News)

https://desertant.com/blog/introducing-desert-ant-labs/

# Summary

Desert Ant Labs, a new European AI company, is launching 18 specialized on-device AI models designed to run locally on phones, tablets, and laptops rather than relying on cloud APIs. The models are small (often just a few MB), extremely fast (processing tasks in milliseconds), and free to use up to 100,000 monthly active devices. Key offerings include Voz for speech transcription (4.7x faster than Whisper), Clear for audio enhancement, and Redact for real-time PII detection across 27 languages.

The company was founded by the team behind the Detail video app, who grew frustrated with cloud API costs and slowness. They argue that on-device intelligence is superior for most routine tasks—such as audio cleaning, photo tagging, and data redaction—since it eliminates per-token costs, reduces latency, keeps user data private, and leverages the vast computing power already available in consumer devices. The models are accessible via SDKs for Swift, Kotlin, and JavaScript, offering a new approach to building AI features that prioritizes efficiency, privacy, and user sovereignty.

### Lotus Notes and the dangers of starting from scratch - (Hacker News)

https://buttondown.com/blog/lotus-notes-email

# Summary

Lotus Notes was a revolutionary collaboration platform launched in 1989 that pioneered features like encryption, rich text formatting, read receipts, and threaded messages—years ahead of standard email. Created by Ray Ozzie to recreate the collaborative environment of PLATO Notes (a 1970s online forum system), Lotus Notes was actually a database-driven platform that supported email as just one feature among many, enabling organizations to build custom applications, forums, calendars, and shared databases that synced across servers.

However, Lotus Notes' greatest strength became its fatal flaw: its custom, non-standard interface designed to work identically across all operating systems violated user conventions and created a steep learning curve. Users complained about counterintuitive shortcuts (ALT+1 to send email instead of CTRL+ENTER), bizarre limitations (only eight windows allowed), and illogical design choices that frustrated anyone switching between Notes and standard applications. While enthusiasts praised its powerful capabilities, the majority of users despised the interface, and the article illustrates how starting from scratch with a proprietary design—rather than following established conventions—ultimately doomed what could have been an even more dominant platform.

# Summary of "Coyote V. Acme"

This humorous New Yorker article, written by Ian Frazier, presents a mock legal complaint filed by Wile E. Coyote against the Acme Company for product liability. Coyote alleges that he has suffered 85 separate injuries from defective Acme products purchased through mail order, including a Rocket Sled, Rocket Skates, explosives, and Spring-Powered Shoes. Each incident is detailed with exaggerated legal precision, describing injuries ranging from extreme elongation of limbs and full-body flattening to singed fur and accordion-like wheezing caused by compression of vertebrae.

The complaint emphasizes Acme's monopoly on specialized predator equipment and argues that the company has recklessly sold dangerous products with inadequate safety warnings or proper functioning mechanisms. Coyote seeks $17 million in punitive damages plus additional compensation for medical expenses and lost income, arguing that Acme's negligence has repeatedly prevented him from earning a living as a professional predator and has severely impacted his quality of life.
