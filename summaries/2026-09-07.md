### De-Brainrot Vacations - (Hacker News)

https://devz.cl/posts/i-spent-my-vacations-de-brainrotting/

# Summary

Daniel reflects on how his brain has become duller over eight years of software engineering work, attributing it to reduced novelty, constant access to dopamine-rich distractions (short videos, games, social media), and a dramatic decline in reading—once his primary intellectual pursuit. He notices his thinking has become slower and lazier despite increased productivity at work.

To combat this "brainrot," Daniel intentionally used his vacation to rebuild intellectual habits, spending time in nature with family while reading four books and unexpectedly discovering a passion for mathematics and physics. He began self-studying calculus, trigonometry, and physics through textbooks and online resources, rediscovering the pure joy of learning for its own sake. While uncertain of the long-term impact, he's already noticed improved mental engagement in his daily life.

### Live map of public transport in Belgium - (Hacker News)

https://openbaarvervoerbelgie.be/

# Summary

**ovlive** is a live map service that displays real-time information about Belgium's public transportation system in one unified interface. It tracks all buses, trams, metros, and trains operated by the four major Belgian transit operators: De Lijn, STIB-MIVB, TEC, and NMBS. The platform provides users with current delays, stops, and departure times across all of Belgium's regions.

The service covers public transit throughout Belgium, including major areas such as Brussels, Antwerp, and the various Flemish and Walloon regions. Users can access information in both Dutch and French, and the website includes sections for FAQs, privacy policies, usage terms, and contact information.

### Caltech Mathathon – first hackathon ever devoted to research level mathematics - (Hacker News)

https://mathathonchallenge.com/index.html

# Caltech Mathathon Summary

The Caltech Mathathon is a 40-hour research competition scheduled for October 30–November 1, 2026, where 100 teams will use frontier AI models to tackle open mathematical conjectures and develop new theories. This event is motivated by recent breakthroughs in AI-assisted mathematics, including the disproof of Erdos's planar unit-distance conjecture (unsolved for 80 years) and the construction of the first explicit non-sofic group (open for 27 years), raising important questions about AI's role in accelerating mathematical discovery and the evolving function of human mathematicians.

Participants will present their results to leading mathematicians for evaluation, with prizes awarded both immediately and after the broader mathematics community has time to verify the findings. Supported by major organizations including OpenAI, Anthropic, and DARPA, this represents the first hackathon dedicated to research-level mathematics, offering $2M+ in AI credits to top competitors.

### Smartphone makers don't bother to comply with EU repairability requirements - (Hacker News)

https://www.theregister.com/personal-tech/2026/09/07/smartphone-makers-dont-bother-to-comply-with-eu-repairability-requirements/5294532

# Summary

One year after EU repairability regulations took effect in June 2025, over 80% of smartphones and tablets sold in Europe still fail to comply with mandatory requirements to provide repair information and pricing. Right to Repair Europe's review found that only 18% of 2,334 smartphone models listed websites with spare parts prices or repair instructions, while many records contained blank fields or linked to irrelevant product pages. Despite this non-compliance, some manufacturers still awarded themselves top marks for repairability.

The report highlights enforcement failures, as no public authority appears to be verifying manufacturer claims, allowing thousands of blank fields to go unnoticed. Critics argue that allowing manufacturers to self-assign repairability scores without publishing supporting documentation undermines the regulations' effectiveness. While this represents a step toward consumer rights, stronger enforcement and mandatory battery replaceability coming in February 2026 will be necessary to make EU repairability rules meaningful.

### Impedance Matching (2017) - (Hacker News)

https://www.edge.org/response-detail/27238

# Summary

W. Daniel Hillis argues that "impedance matching"—a concept describing how systems efficiently transfer energy—should be more widely understood. Impedance matchers are devices or structures that reduce resistance to energy flow between different parts of a system, allowing energy to couple more effectively from one medium to another.

The article provides numerous real-world examples across different domains: car transmissions convert engine rotation into wheel power, foam spikes in recording booths absorb sound waves, trumpet flares project sound into air, and anti-reflective lens coatings minimize light reflection. Hillis even suggests impedance matching concepts could apply to environmental challenges, such as using atmospheric particles to reflect infrared radiation and cool the planet—essentially creating an "impedance mismatch" to prevent unwanted solar energy from heating Earth.

### Speculative Decoding in vLLM on AMD GPUs - (Hacker News)

https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-08-23-speculative-decoding-amd-gpus

# Summary

This article explores **speculative decoding** in vLLM on AMD GPUs, a technique that improves LLM serving throughput by using a lightweight draft component to propose multiple candidate tokens, which are then verified by the target model in a single pass. Unlike standard autoregressive decoding that generates one token per model pass, speculative decoding can commit multiple tokens when several draft predictions are accepted, thereby reducing the number of target-model inference rounds needed.

The article examines five drafting methods—Native MTP, Gemma 4 MTP, EAGLE-3, DFlash, and DSpark—which differ in their architecture and how they receive information from the target model (hidden states, KV cache, or model activations). The post provides practical guidance on enabling speculative decoding in vLLM, reports experimental measurements from AMD Instinct GPUs using ROCm, and discusses tuning considerations for optimizing acceptance behavior and throughput based on specific workloads and model families.

### LG smart TVs caught logging audio with screen off and snooping on local devices - (Hacker News)

https://www.notebookcheck.net/LG-smart-TVs-caught-logging-audio-with-screen-off-and-snooping-on-local-devices.1391214.0.html

# Summary

An investigation by Gamers Nexus revealed that LG smart TVs continuously scan home networks to map connected devices like phones and smartwatches, log microphone audio even when appearing to be powered off, and collect viewing data through Automated Content Recognition (ACR). The captured data—including network names, signal strengths, and location information—is fed to LG Ad Solutions, which claims access to 363 million secondary devices in the US alone. Additionally, researchers discovered the TVs can store voice recordings locally when disconnected from the internet and upload them once reconnected.

The investigation also uncovered remote code execution vulnerabilities in webOS that are under responsible disclosure review. Given these findings, security researchers recommended disconnecting LG televisions from the internet entirely and using external streaming devices instead. As of the article's publication, LG has not commented on these privacy and security concerns.

### 'You Can See Everything' Review: Nathan Fielder's Doc About Elizabeth Holmes - (Hacker News)

https://variety.com/2026/film/reviews/nathan-fielder-surprise-film-telluride-elizabeth-holmes-1236853513/

# Summary

Nathan Fielder's "You Can See Everything" is a 174-minute documentary that follows Elizabeth Holmes, the disgraced Theranos CEO convicted of defrauding investors, during her final 35 days of freedom before beginning her 11-year prison sentence. Fielder employs an unconventional "hangout movie" approach, interviewing Holmes at her beachside home to understand what makes her tick. The documentary reveals Holmes' remarkable ability to deny reality: she maintains she never lied and that her non-existent blood-testing device actually "works," treating the lack of actual chemistry behind it as a minor technical problem rather than fundamental fraud.

What emerges is a portrait of pathological narcissism and delusional thinking. Holmes genuinely believes her own false narrative, exemplified when she fails to understand a basic Abbott and Costello joke, revealing her inability to accept any interpretation of reality beyond her own decree. After Holmes enters prison (where she cannot be interviewed), Fielder brings in actress Amanda Seyfried, who portrayed Holmes in the Hulu series "The Dropout," to read transcripts and sustain the documentary's inquiry into how such dangerous delusion takes hold in seemingly credible people.
